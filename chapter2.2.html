CHAPTER II - REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND SYSTEMS (Page 2)

2.2 Related Systems

Compare similar systems:

System Features Limitations
Amazon Handmade (by Amazon) Provides access to a large customer base, integrated logistics (Fulfillment by Amazon), seller analytics and advertising. High commission fees; strict seller requirements and less focus on small/local artisans.
Etsy (Global Handmade Marketplace) Allows artisans to sell handmade and vintage products; includes product listings, reviews, secure payment system, and global reach. Charges listing and transaction fees, high competition among sellers, limited branding control.
Aseel (Artisan Support Marketplace) Combines e-commerce with support for artisans; promotes handmade goods globally; supports cultural products. Not purely commercial (partly humanitarian), limited scalability, fewer features compared to major platforms.
Felt (Local Handmade Marketplace) Supports local handmade sellers; direct seller-to-buyer transactions; simple and community-focused platform. Limited to a specific region, smaller market reach, fewer advanced features.
Pinkoi (Design & Craft Marketplace) Focuses on creative and handmade products; supports cross-border selling; multi-language platform. High competition; niche focus.

2.3 Synthesis

Based on the reviewed related literature and systems, existing digital marketplaces provide essential features such as product listing, online transactions, and customer interaction that help artisans reach wider markets. However, they also present limitations including high fees, strict requirements, and limited accessibility for small or local makers. In response to these gaps, the proposed system, Likha.ph aims to provide a more accessible, affordable, and user-centered platform tailored specifically for local artisans. Additionally, the use of Agile methodology allows continuous system improvement based on user feedback, making Likha.ph a more inclusive and adaptable digital marketplace compared to existing platforms.
